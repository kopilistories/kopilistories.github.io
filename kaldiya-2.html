The Film

Kaldiya 2

In 2036, before dawn, a young woman arrives at an ancient well in Kachpur.

Five years earlier, a troubled young woman begins her doctoral research into the people who once inhabited the region and the history surrounding an ancient temple whose origins remain obscure. What begins as an academic pursuit gradually draws her into forgotten records, fragments of unfinished research and connections that become increasingly difficult to explain. As the world around her grows harder to inhabit, the research becomes a place where she can retreat from it.

Around Kachpur, the trajectories of four lives gradually begin to intersect.

The World

Kachpur

Kachpur lies in the lower Brahmaputra valley, shaped by rivers, fields and dense forest. In winter, the Kaldiya recedes, exposing long stretches of sand and earth along its banks. Deep inside the forest, on the other side of the Kaldiya, stands an ancient temple, largely reclaimed by the vegetation around it.

For generations, people have crossed the Kaldiya without going further. The forest has slowly reclaimed the temple and the stories that once surrounded it. Local traditions speak of an ancient people who once inhabited these forests and of the beliefs they left behind. Beyond these fragments, little is known.

Around it, winter moves quietly through the landscape. Rice fields turn golden towards harvest. Morning mist settles over the lowlands, while the soft sun of the season gives way quickly to the long shadows of evening.

Winter Light

Winter Light

Winter arrives quietly in Kachpur.

The Kaldiya withdraws from the land, leaving behind long stretches of pale sand and exposed earth. Mist lingers over the fields in the early hours, slowly giving way to the soft light of the morning. Rice turns gold beneath the low winter sun. By late afternoon, the light begins to thin, and the forest gathers its shadows.

The landscape simply becomes quieter...more distant, more withdrawn.

The film follows that light through the changing hours of the day, allowing the landscape to carry what the characters leave unspoken.

The Characters

Four lives.
Four ways of moving forward.

Prof. Jishnu Bharadwaj

Prof. Jishnu Bharadwaj is a respected local historian at Uttar Kachpur College, known for his work on the history of the region. He has built a life beyond the events of the past. He has a family, a child and the familiar rhythms of an academic life. Yet some things do not disappear simply because life continues around them.

Dr. Ananta Nath Dutta

Ananta Nath Dutta teaches physics at a rural college and has gradually withdrawn from almost everything beyond the narrow routine of his work. Years have passed, but his life has contracted rather than moved forward.

Niharika

Niharika begins her doctoral work in history, drawn to the people, traditions and fragments of a past that have survived around Kachpur. Her research is careful and deliberate, but beyond it, the contours of her life are becoming increasingly difficult to hold together. As the world around her grows harder to inhabit, the work becomes a place where she can retreat from it.

Dr. Aneta Wojnar

Aneta Wojnar is a physicist and researcher at the University of Wrocław, pursuing questions at the frontiers of fundamental physics. Far from Kachpur, she follows a question of her own. Time, however, has a way of weaving distant threads together.
